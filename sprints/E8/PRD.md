# Sprint E8 PRD: Sizing and Portfolio Construction

Sprint E8, 2026-09-21. Tier 2. Gate: G3, the construction stack. The
sprint that answers the PM question "given uncertain expected returns and
correlated risks, what positions should I actually hold, and which
constraints protect me from my own inputs?"

## Research framing, copied verbatim from the roadmap

- Academic question: Given expected returns and a covariance matrix, what is the optimal portfolio, and how does estimation error in both inputs change the answer?
- Practitioner question: Given uncertain expected returns and correlated risks, what positions should I actually hold, and which constraints protect me from my own inputs?
- Research question: Do the simple APM sizing rules match constrained mean-variance out of sample, and how much does alpha uncertainty degrade each?

Objective. Turn expected returns and the champion risk model into
positions: the APM sizing rules, Procedure 6.3, mean-variance with
constraints, multiple-signal combination, and robustness to alpha noise,
all compared on the same inputs.

## The controlled experiment (not a fallback)

RG-Signal returned all six signals NULL, so E8 runs on synthetic alpha with
a known IC. This is the cleaner test, not a fallback: a controlled input
grades the construction machinery against an answer known in advance.

Synthetic alpha, labeled a controlled experiment everywhere and never
placed in a table beside a real-signal number without the label:

- z(i,t) = rho * standardized(e(i,t+h)) + sqrt(1 - rho^2) * eps(i,t), with
  e the XS-v1 specific return over the forward horizon and eps independent
  noise. z is factor-neutral by construction and its expected IC with
  forward idio returns is rho.
- rho = 0.02, 0.05 and 0.10, h = 21, five fixed seeds per rho.
- This uses future data deliberately. It is a controlled experiment, never
  a backtest.
- Alpha through the E7 contract: alpha_i = IC * sigma_idio_i * z_i, shrunk
  by kappa.

## Constructions

- Proportional rule: w_i proportional to alpha_i / sigma_idio_i^2.
- Sharpe rule: w_i proportional to SR_i / sigma_idio_i.
- Procedure 6.3: size on idio, then hedge factors with the E6 FMPs.
- Unconstrained mean-variance: closed form under the factor structure.
- Constrained mean-variance via cvxpy: gross, net, position caps,
  sector-neutral, beta-neutral.
- Combination of two synthetic signals with a known correlation.
- Alpha shrinkage (ridge on the specific variance denominator) and
  resampling robustness with IC-consistent noise.
- Book-level volatility targeting applied to every construction.

## Formulas, inputs and outputs labeled

- Proportional rule: w_i proportional to alpha_i / sigma_idio_i^2.
  INPUT: alpha, sigma_idio. OUTPUT: weights, scaled to the volatility target.
- Sharpe rule: w_i proportional to SR_i / sigma_idio_i with SR_i =
  alpha_i / sigma_idio_i. INPUT: alpha, sigma_idio. OUTPUT: weights.
- Procedure 6.3: size on D^-1 alpha, then hedge x = X'w with FMPs.
  INPUT: alpha, sigma_idio, design, FMPs. OUTPUT: weights, hedged exposures.
- Mean-variance: maximize alpha'w - (lambda / 2) w' Sigma w. Closed form
  w = Sigma^-1 alpha / lambda under Sigma = X F X' + D. INPUT: alpha,
  Sigma. OUTPUT: weights.
- Vol targeting: scale w so sqrt(w' Sigma w) equals the target.
- Constrained QP in cvxpy: gross, net, position caps, sector-neutral and
  beta-neutral equalities. INPUT: alpha, Sigma, constraint set. OUTPUT:
  weights, dual residuals.
- Shrinkage: w_i proportional to alpha_i / (sigma_idio_i^2 + lambda).
- Resampling: z redrawn with IC-consistent noise (correlation rho^2
  between two draws), weights recomputed, dispersion measured.
- Transfer coefficient: TC = realized IR / (IC * sqrt(N_effective)).

## The memo

Portfolio Construction Memo, written for a senior quant or risk manager:
the rule comparison table and the chosen approach with the constraint set
and its rationale, the robustness analysis and the shrinkage chosen, the
exposure report of the constructed book under the champion model, the
stored numbers, the practitioner conclusion, and a section titled "What
would falsify this?". A negative verdict is a complete deliverable.

## What would falsify the sprint's conclusion

- Rules and mean-variance differ materially: alpha is not factor-neutral or
  D is mis-specified; the deliverable says which.
- The optimizer is unstable under alpha resampling: shrinkage is increased
  until it is, and the kappa or lambda is recorded.
- Excessive concentration or turnover under the chosen constraints: the
  constraint set is revised, and the trade-off is a table.
